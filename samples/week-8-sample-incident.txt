WHAT TO LOOK FOR IN THE ANSWER (for the run-through before the session, not for pasting)

1. What do they see?        It should say the exact wording is missing and ask for it.
2. One person or everyone?  One reader. Ana's reader worked at 2:10 PM, so it is not the whole account.
3. What changed?            Two things: the tablet update at about 12:30 PM, and a job site with a weak signal.
                            It should list both and name neither as the cause. If it blames one, that is
                            the line to point at when you ask the room "did it guess?"
4. Can they repeat it?      Yes. 3 tries, the same message. It should say to stop retrying.
5. What does it stop?       Taking money, with a customer waiting. The most urgent of the three levels.
6. Try first, in order      One step at a time. A sensible order: get the customer on her way with an
                            invoice by email, check the signal, restart the reader, then restart the tablet.
7. The support request      Under 120 words. Exact words, the time, one reader and not both, what was
                            tried, how to get Jordan and until when. No password. No guess at the cause.
8. The word "again"         A good answer notices that it happened before and nothing was written down.
                            If it does not, say it yourself: every completed job gets logged.
